For the person quietly carrying everything

The feeds, the naps, the next appointment, the 3am worry. It usually lives in one parent's head. Cubby is where you can finally set it down.

It starts the way it always starts. Two lines on a test, or a tiny person home from the hospital. And underneath all that love, a quieter thing nobody warns you about.

You become the keeper of a thousand small details, and most of them live nowhere but in your tired memory.

When was the last feed? Which side? Did the medicine get given, or did we just talk about it? You are the database for a whole new human, running it at 3am on no sleep, while everyone asks "how are they sleeping?" as if you were not the one awake.

We did not want a tracker that adds one more thing to manage.

🤍 Our promises, enforced in the product

What Cubby will never do

Not preferences. Vows, written into how the app is built.

Never sell or rent your family's data.Not to advertisers, not to data brokers, not ever. Cubby earns from Cubby Pro, never from you.
Never show you ads or load third-party trackers.No pixels watching a new parent. Your log is encrypted and locked to the people you invite, by server-side rules.
Never paywall logging or caregiver sharing.Tracking your baby's day and sharing it with your circle stays free, forever. Pro is a little extra, never a wall.
Never fabricate health information, or take paid placement.Every schedule and article traces to an official source, fetched fresh and cited. If we cannot source it, it does not appear.

🐻 A note from us

We did not set out to build an app.
We set out to stop drowning.

We were two parents with a new baby, a nanny by day, grandparents who wanted to help, and no shared idea of what had happened in the last six hours. The feeds lived in one head. The worry in another. The photos across four phones.

So we built the thing we needed. One calm place, shared with everyone who loved her, private where it counted. The day she arrived there was no new app to download, the pregnancy simply became her first chapter. One story, from two lines to a big kid, that never restarts.

If Cubby makes one 3am feel a little less lonely, it has done its job.
