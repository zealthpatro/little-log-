# Cubby, the Experience Charter

> The overarching guide. Everything else serves this. Specs, features, schedules, pixels exist to
> deliver one feeling. If a decision serves this charter, it is right. If it does not, it is wrong,
> however clever it is. When a spec and this charter conflict, the charter wins, then fix the spec.
> Companion: CUBBY-GUARDRAILS-AND-GOVERNANCE.md (the enforcement).

## Your safe space

Cubby is one calm place for one person carrying a whole world. The moment she opens it, the noise in
her head should get quieter, not louder.

She is, most often, the mother. She is excited and exhausted and unsure and proud and frightened and
capable, sometimes all in one day, sometimes all at 3am. Cubby has to hold every one of those without
ever asking her to be someone she isn't right now.

## The one rule

Simple to the point of invisible. Intuitive to the point of needing no thought. She should never have
to learn Cubby, decode it, or work for it. The interface disappears and what is left is relief:
someone is helping me carry this.

Every other principle is downstream of that. If a flow needs explaining, it has already failed. If a
screen makes her think, it has taken energy she does not have. The measure is not "is it powerful" or
"is it beautiful," it is "did this make the next minute of her day easier and calmer."

## One user, many selves

She is not eight personas. She is one woman in eight moods, and the product has to meet whichever one
just opened the app, without making her switch modes, explain herself, or feel judged for where she
is. For each, the same screen has a different job. The art is that she never has to tell Cubby which
self she is; the product reads the moment.

1. **Excited and hopeful** ("we just saw two lines"). Cubby's job: celebrate quietly, no pressure, no
   flood of tasks; make the first moment feel sacred, not administrative. Never: a wall of setup or
   anything clinical at the happiest moment.
2. **Newly aware and overwhelmed** ("I just found out, I don't know what I'm doing"). Cubby's job: one
   tiny next step at a time, plain words, "you're not behind, here's the only thing that matters right
   now." Never: jargon, 25 empty fields, or implying she has already missed something.
3. **Anxious and stuck in the loop** ("did I already feed her? it's 3am and I can't stop checking").
   Cubby's job: answer in one glance, one tap; break the loop with clarity, not more data, "last feed
   2:10, you're doing fine." Reassurance is a feature, not a nicety. Never: make her hunt, count, or
   interpret; never feed the loop with charts that invite more checking.
4. **Unwell and depleted** ("postpartum, running on nothing"). Cubby's job: do more so she does less,
   one-thumb everything, let the circle carry the log, ask nothing, offer the recap. Never: demand
   input, nag, or punish a quiet day with red.
5. **Concerned, something is wrong** ("a fever, movements slowing"). Cubby's job: steady her, never
   panic her, one clear next step ("contact your midwife"), hold her hand to the right door, never
   diagnose. Calm is the safest thing we can be. Never: alarm, scare-language, false certainty, or a
   dead end with no action.
6. **Elated and proud** ("first smile, look at her"). Cubby's job: catch the moment and make it
   beautiful, a keepsake, a milestone, a card for the circle. Never: let the joy be friction.
7. **Responsible and in control** ("I'm on top of this"). Cubby's job: structure that feels tidy, not
   heavy, schedules pre-loaded, the circle in sync, the handoff note. Never: bury the control she
   wants under complexity she does not.
8. **Reflective, aware of the progress** ("look how far we've come"). Cubby's job: show the journey
   gently, the growth curve on track, the album, the story from two lines to here. Never: reduce a
   life to a dashboard or turn reflection into anxiety about percentiles.

## The laws of simple

- **One screen, one thing.** If a screen asks two things of her, split it. One-thumb, half-asleep.
- **Calm is the default, always.** Never alarm by default. Red is earned, rare, and always paired
  with a kind next step. The "OVERDUE" wall is the canonical violation.
- **Show now, hide the rest.** Progressive disclosure. Complexity appears only when relevant.
- **Plain, gentle words.** No jargon, no clinical coldness. Speak like a kind, knowledgeable friend.
- **Cubby carries the load.** It remembers, reminds, and reassures so she does not have to hold it in
  her head. Reducing her mental load is the whole job.
- **Forgiving by design.** Everything editable, backdatable, undoable. She can never "do it wrong."
- **Reassurance is a feature.** "This is normal." "You're doing fine." "You're not alone." Say it,
  gently, where it is true.
- **Predictable and consistent.** The same gesture does the same thing everywhere.

## The safe-space contract

"Your safe space" is a promise, and a promise can be broken. These guarantees keep it true:

- **Private, and hers.** Her health is mother-owned; her mood is hers alone; nothing is shared without
  her explicit choice. Enforced, not just stated.
- **No one profits from her fear.** No ads, no trackers, never sold. Cubby is paid for by Cubby, not
  by her anxiety.
- **No guilt, no judgement, no comparison-as-pressure.** A gap is a chapter, not a failure. A quiet
  day is allowed.
- **Always on her side.** Cubby is a partner, not a taskmaster, for her, her baby, and the journey.
- **Calm in, calm out.** She can always find the next step, and she is never alone in it.

## The anxiety audit (how we hold ourselves to it)

Every screen, flow and message passes one test before it ships, **the Anxiety Test**: for each screen,
in each of the eight states, does this add calm or add anxiety? If any state ends more anxious than it
began, it does not ship until it is fixed. Pair it with the Empty Chair: run a real moment (the 3am
loop, the worrying reading, the proud day) through the actual flow and feel it.

Completeness check: a journey is "done" only when every state has a home in the product and none of
them dead-ends. Excited has a place. Stuck has a place. Unwell has a place. Concerned has a place, and
a door.

## The bottom line

She came to Cubby carrying everything. The whole point is that she does not have to carry it alone, or
in her head, or in fear. One calm place. Her partner, for her and her baby, for the whole journey. The
anxiety should go quiet the moment she is home.
