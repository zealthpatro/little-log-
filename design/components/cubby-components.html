Anatomy

  1. Header. Name, age, and the freshness line. The freshness line is not decoration: a widget refreshes on iOS's budget, not on demand, so it must say how old it is.
  2. Status row. Three StatusCells divided by --line. Three, never four: a fourth halves the number size and the number is the whole point.
  3. Action row. Three ActionButtons in the app's own order, feed then sleep then nappy, matching QUICK_ACTIONS so the widget and the app never disagree about which three matter.

Properties

Figma descriptionThree things she can read at a glance and three she can tap, for a baby at home. Statuses read "not yet" until she has logged one, and the widget says how long ago it refreshed rather than pretending to be live.
DoShow "not yet" when there is no entry. An empty widget that admits it is empty is honest; one showing a stale number is not.
Don'tAdd a fourth statistic, a streak, a daily total or a target. The Anxiety Test: none of them help at 3am, and a streak punishes the night she could not log.

Figma descriptionQuiet by default: shows the app's name and nothing about the pregnancy. She can turn on one fact, her week and her next visit, and only she can. Kicks appear at 28 weeks and contractions at 36, the same gates the app applies.

Why the default is silence

"Week 9" on a home screen tells a room full of people about a first-trimester pregnancy before she has. The post-birth widget has no equivalent risk: a baby is already news.

All four pregnancy quick actions are ownerOnly in the rules, and two are week-gated. A static list cannot check who is holding the phone or how many weeks it has been, so the component carries those as properties and the default answers no.

Rejected, and kept

Will not build

Kept on the page on purpose. A rejected design with its reason attached stops the same idea arriving again in six months as a fresh suggestion.

IdeaWhy not
Pregnancy multi-status grid: kicks, blood pressure, weight, contractions, moodIt tells the room. It is a medical dashboard on glass, which DESIGN.md names as what Cubby is not. Four of the five are owner-only. Two are empty before 28 and 36 weeks, and "0 kicks" is a frightening way to say "too early".
A streak, a daily total, or a target on the post-birth widgetFails the Anxiety Test. None of them help at 3am, and a streak punishes the night she could not log.
A fourth statistic on the medium widgetHalves the number size. The number is the whole reason to look.
Pregnancy on the lock screen, in any formA lock screen renders without unlocking. It is the one surface she cannot take back.

Publishing this to Figma

When there is a Cubby team to publish into, this is the order. Nothing here is a design decision; they are all made above.

  1. Variables first. One collection, two modes, Day and Night, with the names in Foundations. Components bind to variables; nothing is a raw hex.
  2. Parts next. StatusCell, ActionButton, Header, each with the variant properties listed against it and its description string pasted in verbatim.
  3. Then the widgets, assembled from instances of the parts, never redrawn.
  4. Then the rejected page, as documentation inside the file, so the reasons travel with the components.
  5. Values come from the repo, not from this page and not from taste. If they disagree, app/index.html is right and both this page and Figma are stale.
