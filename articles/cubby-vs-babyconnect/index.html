Cubby vs BabyConnect: a direct comparison

All ages · Comparison · Reviewed 11 June 2026 · All articles

Disclosure: Cubby is the publisher of this article. We have done our best to represent both apps fairly and accurately as of June 2026. If you spot anything out of date, please reach out via our contact page.

Choosing a baby tracking app in the early weeks can feel surprisingly important. When sleep deprivation is high and the details blur together, a log that is quick to tap and easy to share with your partner or carer makes a real difference. This article looks at two popular options, Cubby and BabyConnect, across the areas that matter most: what they track, how they handle privacy, how caregiver sharing works, what they cost, and the overall experience of using them day to day.

What each app tracks

Both Cubby and BabyConnect cover the core categories new parents rely on most: feeds (breast, bottle and solids), sleep, nappy changes, weight and height measurements, and notes or mood entries. The depth within each category differs, however.

  • Feeds: Cubby logs breast-feeding with a timer per side, bottle volumes, and solids once weaning begins. BabyConnect also tracks nursing duration and bottle amounts, and adds fields for pumping sessions, which some parents find useful.
  • Sleep: Both apps record sleep start and end times with total duration. Cubby presents sleep history in a calm visual timeline; BabyConnect offers a chart view and lets parents categorise naps separately from night sleep.
  • Nappies: Wet, dirty and combination entries are supported in both, with optional notes.
  • Health and milestones: BabyConnect has dedicated fields for temperature, medication doses and doctor appointments. Cubby keeps health notes in the general log and focuses milestone tracking on developmental events rather than medical records.
  • Vaccine schedule: Cubby includes a built-in country-specific immunisation schedule so upcoming jabs appear alongside the daily log. BabyConnect does not include a built-in vaccine schedule; parents who want that integration will need a separate reminder.
  • Growth: Both apps plot weight and length over time, and both support WHO growth percentile charts.

Privacy and data handling

Privacy is a significant concern for many parents because baby tracking data is personal and sensitive. The two apps take different approaches.

Cubby was built around the idea that your child's data belongs to your family. Data is not used for advertising, not sold to third parties, and the product is funded by subscriptions rather than by monetising user information. The privacy policy is written in plain language and is available on the website.

BabyConnect is a paid app without an advertising model, which is a good starting point. The app has been available since around 2010 and has a long track record. Parents who want to read the precise data-handling terms should review BabyConnect's privacy policy directly, as the specifics can change over time.

For parents who place privacy at the top of their criteria, Cubby's explicit privacy-first positioning and subscription funding model offer a clear answer.

Caregiver sharing

One of the most practical features in any baby tracker is the ability to share the log in real time with a partner, grandparent, nanny or anyone else involved in care. This means no duplicate questions about the last feed or nap.

  • Cubby is designed so that every authorised carer sees the same live log. Adding a carer takes a few taps, and each person uses the app under their own account. There is no limit on the number of carers on any plan.
  • BabyConnect also supports multiple carers. The app allows different carers to log entries from their own devices, and all entries appear in the shared timeline. BabyConnect has long been respected for its multi-carer capability and was one of the earlier apps to offer it.

Both apps handle the core sharing requirement well. The difference is mainly in the setup experience: Cubby aims to make onboarding a new carer as quick as possible, while BabyConnect gives more configuration options that some families appreciate and others find fiddly.

Pricing

Pricing structures shift over time, so always check each app's current listing for the most accurate figures.

  • Cubby has a free tier that covers feeds, sleep and nappy tracking for a single baby. Cubby Pro adds advanced reporting, multi-baby support and priority support, available as a monthly or annual subscription. There is no upfront purchase cost to get started.
  • BabyConnect is a paid download on both iOS and Android. There is no separate free tier; the purchase gives access to all features without an ongoing subscription. This means a known one-time cost but no incremental feature unlocks.

Which pricing model suits you depends on how long you plan to use the app. A one-time purchase can be good value over a year or more; a free-tier-plus-subscription model lets you try before committing any money.

Design and day-to-day experience

Both apps are available on iOS and Android. The experience of opening the app twenty times a day during the first weeks matters more than any individual feature list.

Cubby is designed around a single clear log view. The intention is to make each entry as fast as possible: open the app, tap the right activity, done. The visual style is calm and low-contrast, which parents who are often using their phone in the dark report as welcome. It is also a progressive web app (PWA), which means it works on any device with a browser without requiring a separate download, useful for carers who are not on the same phone platform.

BabyConnect has a functional interface that has been refined over many years. It offers more configuration depth, which is useful for parents who want to customise categories or set up detailed notes templates. Some users find the additional options helpful; others prefer a simpler starting point and add complexity only if needed.

Summary: which app is right for you?

There is no single correct answer. Both apps are capable and have been used by many families. The choice comes down to priorities:

  • If privacy and data ethics are your top concern, Cubby's explicit privacy-first approach and subscription business model are worth the extra confidence.
  • If you want a built-in vaccine schedule alongside your daily tracking, Cubby is the only one of the two that includes this.
  • If you prefer a one-time payment and full feature access from day one, BabyConnect's paid download model is straightforward.
  • If you value a simple, fast tap-to-log experience that works across any device without a separate app install, Cubby's PWA design is an advantage.
  • If you want detailed health logging including medication and temperature fields, BabyConnect's wider set of built-in categories may fit better.

Frequently asked questions

What is the main difference between Cubby and BabyConnect?

Cubby is a privacy-first tracker built around a calm, distraction-free experience with a built-in vaccine schedule. BabyConnect is a long-established app with broad tracking categories and a web dashboard. Cubby focuses on simplicity and data privacy; BabyConnect targets families who want a highly configurable log.

Is Cubby free to use?

Cubby offers a free tier that covers the core tracking features for feeds, sleep and nappies. Cubby Pro unlocks additional features such as advanced reports and priority support, available on a monthly or annual subscription.

Which app is better for sharing with a nanny or grandparent?

Both apps support caregiver sharing. Cubby is designed from the ground up so that every person who cares for the baby can see the same real-time log without needing to set up complex permissions, and there is no cap on the number of carers you can add.

Does Cubby track the vaccine schedule?

Yes. Cubby includes a country-specific vaccine schedule so parents can see upcoming immunisations alongside the daily tracking log, all in one place. BabyConnect does not include a built-in vaccine schedule.

Which app is better for privacy-conscious parents?

Cubby was built with privacy as a core principle. Baby data is not sold or used for advertising, and the business is funded by subscriptions rather than by monetising user data. Parents who want clear answers about where their child's data goes will find Cubby's approach more transparent.
