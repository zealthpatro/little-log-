Your baby at 10 months: development, play and what to expect

Development · Updated July 2026 · All articles

The approach to a first birthday is a remarkable time. Your 10-month-old is moving more confidently, communicating with growing intention, feeding themselves with real skill and becoming a fascinating little person with clear preferences, opinions and a growing sense of humour. The changes between 9 and 12 months feel rapid and are genuinely worth savouring.

This guide covers what most babies are doing around the 10-month mark. As always, developmental timelines are ranges, not prescriptions. What matters is a general pattern of progress, not hitting every milestone at an exact date. Speak to your health visitor or GP if you have specific concerns.

Motor development: on the move and heading up

Mobility is expanding rapidly as your baby's strength, balance and confidence grow.

  • Walking with support. Most 10-month-olds are walking when holding onto furniture or your hands. Some are cruising confidently along the sofa; others are still mastering this. Independent first steps can come anywhere from 9 to 15 months, so there is a very wide normal range.
  • Pulling to stand and lowering back down. Many babies at this age are getting better at not just pulling up but also lowering themselves back to sitting, which takes a different kind of muscle control.
  • Crawling as confident transport. For babies who crawl, it is now a fast and reliable way to get where they want to go. Some babies are so efficient at crawling they delay walking for a while because crawling simply works well.

Fine motor: the pincer grasp is here

By 10 months the pincer grasp is mostly established for many babies, opening up a whole new world of fine motor exploration.

  • Picking up small objects. Your baby can pick up small pieces of food, crumbs and small objects with a neat thumb-and-forefinger grasp. This makes self-feeding finger foods much more successful.
  • Deliberate placing and releasing. As well as picking things up, your baby is practising letting them go in a chosen place. Dropping objects into a container is a skill many 10-month-olds enjoy and practise repeatedly.
  • Pointing begins. The index finger point is one of the most exciting milestones of this period. Your baby starts using their pointing finger to direct your attention to something interesting. This is a huge communication step.

Communication: pointing and joint attention

The shift from pure babbling toward intentional communication is one of the defining developments of this period.

  • Pointing is a crucial milestone. When your baby points, they are not just indicating an object. They are inviting you to share their interest in it. This shared attention, looking at the same thing together, is called joint attention and is one of the most important precursors to language development.
  • Waving hello and goodbye. Many 10-month-olds have mastered the social wave, understanding it as a greeting and farewell signal. Some do it reliably; others wave mainly when prompted.
  • Following a point. Your baby is also getting better at following your point to look at what you are showing them, rather than just looking at your finger. This two-way shared attention is a key building block for conversation.

Language: first words may be emerging

The boundary between babble and first words is delightfully blurry at this stage.

  • First words can emerge from 9 to 15 months. Some 10-month-olds have one or two consistent words; many are still in the rich babbling phase. Both are normal. A word counts if your baby uses the same sound consistently to refer to the same person, object or situation.
  • Understanding far ahead of speaking. Your baby's understanding (receptive language) is well ahead of what they can say. They likely understand several dozen words, even if they are not saying any of them yet.
  • Communicative babble. Even without words, your baby is using babble communicatively, varying tone to express different intentions like wanting something, protesting, or asking a question.

Feeding: three meals and finger food independence

Mealtimes at 10 months are becoming real family affairs.

  • Three meals a day. Most families have moved to three small solid meals per day by 10 months, alongside breast milk or formula. Snacks may also be introduced.
  • Texture close to family food. Your baby can manage minced, chopped or mashed versions of most of what the family eats, with a few exceptions such as honey, whole nuts and very salty foods.
  • Finger foods at every meal. With a reliable pincer grasp, offering finger foods at every meal supports independence and fine motor development at the same time. Let your baby feed themselves as much as possible.
  • A cup for drinks. Continuing to offer water in an open or free-flow cup helps establish the cup as the primary drink vessel, which is the direction things need to go after the first birthday.

Sleep: two naps, most days

Most 10-month-olds are on two naps a day, one in the late morning and one in the early afternoon. The transition to one nap usually happens somewhere between 13 and 18 months, though a few babies do it earlier. Night sleep varies enormously between babies at this age.

Play ideas for a 10-month-old

Play at 10 months reflects your baby's growing motor skills, curiosity and social awareness.

  • Containers and sorting. Dropping objects into a container and taking them out again is enormously satisfying at this age. Simple stacking cups, rings or a box with a slot cut in the lid all work well.
  • Cause-and-effect toys. Push-button toys, pop-up toys and simple musical instruments that reward an action with a sound or movement are ideal for this stage.
  • Bath play with pouring toys. Cups and small jugs for pouring water back and forth are some of the best bath toys for 10-month-olds, building fine motor skills while being genuinely fun.
  • Board books together. Looking at simple board books together, naming pictures and pointing, is wonderful for language and joint attention at the same time.
  • Cruising challenges. If your baby is cruising, placing a toy slightly further along the sofa encourages them to practise their supported walking.

When to talk to your health visitor or GP

Mention it to your health visitor if your baby:

  • Is not pointing, waving or using any gestures by around 12 months
  • Does not respond to their name
  • Is not babbling with varied sounds
  • Is not standing with support at all
  • Has lost skills they previously had

Pointing and joint attention are particularly important milestones to watch. If your baby is not pointing or following a point by around 12 months, it is worth raising with your health visitor.

Frequently asked questions

Should my 10-month-old be walking?

Most 10-month-olds are not yet walking independently. Walking with support or cruising along furniture is typical at this age. Independent walking can arrive anywhere from 9 to 15 months, and all of those timelines are normal.

Why is pointing such an important milestone?

Pointing is a major communication milestone because it shows your baby can share attention with you about something in the world, which is called joint attention. It is also a key precursor to language development.

When should I expect my baby's first word?

First words can emerge anywhere from about 9 to 15 months. Some babies say their first recognisable word around 10 months; others are still in the babbling phase at 12 months. A wide range is normal. If there are no words at all by 16 months, mention it to your health visitor.

What finger foods are safe for a 10-month-old?

Soft cooked vegetables cut into small pieces, ripe banana, soft cooked pasta, small pieces of well-cooked egg and toast strips are all good options. Avoid anything hard, round or sticky that could be a choking risk. Always supervise mealtimes.

What does joint attention mean?

Joint attention is when a baby and a caregiver both focus on the same object or event at the same time, often prompted by a point or a gaze. It is a foundational skill for language and social development, emerging between about 9 and 12 months.

Is separation anxiety normal at 10 months?

Yes. Separation anxiety is very common between 8 and 18 months. At 10 months your baby fully understands that you exist even when you are not in the room, which makes your absence genuinely hard for them. Consistent, calm goodbyes and reassuring returns help over time.
