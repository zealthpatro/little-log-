Frequently asked questions

What should I eat in the first trimester?

Focus on folate-rich foods such as leafy greens, beans and fortified cereals, alongside a 400 microgram folic acid supplement. Iron, calcium and vitamin D are also important throughout pregnancy. Small, frequent meals can help if nausea is making it hard to eat larger portions. Avoid raw or undercooked meat, unpasteurised cheeses, raw shellfish and high-mercury fish.

Is anxiety normal in pregnancy?

Yes. Feeling anxious at times is very common in pregnancy, particularly in the first trimester and again near the end. Some worry is a natural part of caring about your baby. If anxiety feels overwhelming, is affecting your sleep or daily life, or if you are having persistent intrusive thoughts, speak to your midwife or GP. Effective support is available and asking for help early makes a real difference.

When will I have my 12-week scan?

The NHS dating scan is usually offered between 10 and 14 weeks, most commonly around 12 weeks. It confirms your due date and, together with a blood test, screens for conditions including Down's syndrome. You will receive your appointment through your midwife after your booking visit.

What symptoms are normal in early pregnancy?

Nausea, tiredness, breast tenderness, needing to wee more often and a heightened sense of smell are all common in early pregnancy. Light spotting around the time of implantation can happen and is usually nothing to worry about. Severe vomiting, heavy bleeding, one-sided pain or a high temperature are symptoms that need prompt medical attention.

How do I prepare for labour?

Writing a birth plan helps you think through your preferences for pain relief, positions, and who you want in the room. Packing your hospital bag from about 36 weeks is a good idea. Your midwife can talk you through what to expect and antenatal classes, whether NHS or independent, give you and your birth partner practical preparation.

What foods should I avoid in pregnancy?

The NHS advises avoiding raw or undercooked meat, raw shellfish, unpasteurised soft cheeses (such as brie and camembert), pate, and high-mercury fish including shark, swordfish and marlin. Limit oily fish to two portions a week and tuna to four cans a week. The advice is to avoid alcohol entirely. Limit caffeine to 200 mg a day, roughly one to two cups of coffee.
