Cubby vs Huckleberry (2026): a direct comparison

All ages · Tools & comparisons · Reviewed 10 June 2026 · All articles

Cubby and Huckleberry are two of the most frequently compared baby tracker apps. Both let you log feeds, sleep, and nappy changes. Both have a free tier. But they are designed around quite different core assumptions about what parents need most, and the right choice depends heavily on where you live and what you are trying to solve. This page puts them side by side.

Disclosure: Cubby is the publisher of this article. We have tried to represent Huckleberry accurately based on publicly available information, but Huckleberry's features and pricing change. Read Huckleberry's own site before deciding. Details verified June 2026.

Where Huckleberry is stronger: sleep prediction

Huckleberry's SweetSpot is a genuinely useful feature that Cubby does not replicate. It analyses your baby's observed wake windows and sleep patterns to recommend a specific time window to attempt a nap. For families in the 0-6 month range where unpredictable naps cause significant stress, this kind of prediction can be a practical lifesaver.

SweetSpot requires a Huckleberry PLUS subscription. The free tier of Huckleberry logs sleep but does not include the predictive feature. If sleep scheduling is your dominant concern in the first few months, Huckleberry PLUS is worth evaluating seriously.

Where Cubby is stronger: vaccine tracking and UK families

Cubby is purpose-built to be used outside the US as well as within it. The UK NHS schedule, US CDC schedule, UAE MOHAP schedule, and German STIKO schedule are all included, with due-date reminders based on your baby's birth date. You see which jabs are overdue, which are coming up, and can log each one as it is given.

Huckleberry does not include UK vaccine schedules. If you are a UK parent and vaccine tracking matters to you, this is a clear differentiator. Missing an NHS jab is a real risk for families juggling a newborn without reminders; Cubby is designed specifically to solve that.

Privacy and advertising

Cubby does not show ads at any tier, does not use third-party analytics, and does not sell user data. This is a design choice, not a marketing claim: the app does not include ad SDKs or trackers.

Huckleberry's free tier includes advertising. You should read Huckleberry's current privacy policy for details on data practices. When you are logging health data about your infant, this is worth understanding before you commit to an app.

Family sharing

Both apps support multiple caregivers. In Cubby, this is included at no extra cost: all members of a household share the same real-time log. When your partner logs a 3am feed, you see it when you wake up; when you give a nappy change, the baby's day-view updates for everyone.

Check Huckleberry's current plan structure for how they handle multi-user sharing, as this has varied between plans.

The app experience

Cubby is a progressive web app (PWA): it installs from the browser onto your phone's home screen, works offline, and receives updates automatically without requiring an app store release. This means Cubby works on any smartphone, including those that cannot access certain app stores.

Huckleberry is available as a native iOS and Android app. Native apps can feel slightly more polished on their target platform; the trade-off is that PWAs work across more devices and platforms without gatekeeping.

Who should use each app?

This is not a case where one app is objectively better. They solve slightly different problems:

  • If you are in the UK, want NHS vaccine reminders, or care about a no-ad, no-tracker privacy commitment, Cubby is the right default.
  • If sleep scheduling via predictive AI suggestions is your top priority in the first few months, and you are happy to pay for that feature, Huckleberry PLUS is worth a trial.
  • If you are in the US and primarily need a sleep-focused tracker with polished native app design, Huckleberry fits that.
  • If you want your whole family sharing the same data without extra costs or complexity, Cubby handles that by default.

Frequently asked questions

What is the main difference between Cubby and Huckleberry?

The biggest practical difference is their focus. Huckleberry is built around sleep analysis and its SweetSpot nap-timing feature. Cubby is built around whole-family tracking with vaccine reminders, no-ad privacy, and international schedule support. They suit different priorities.

Is Huckleberry free?

Huckleberry has a free tier that covers basic logging. SweetSpot requires a paid PLUS subscription. The free tier includes advertising.

Does Cubby have a SweetSpot equivalent?

Cubby logs your baby's sleep history so you can see patterns, but it does not have a direct predictive nap-window tool. If AI-driven sleep scheduling is your top priority, Huckleberry PLUS has the stronger feature for that specific use case.

Which is better for UK families?

Cubby is purpose-built for UK families. It includes the NHS vaccination schedule, sends reminders for upcoming jabs, and draws its article content from NHS guidance. Huckleberry does not include UK vaccine schedules.

Does Huckleberry sell data?

Check Huckleberry's own current privacy policy for the definitive answer. Cubby's commitment is explicit: no third-party analytics, no advertising, no data sales. When logging sensitive health data about your baby, always check the privacy policy of any app you use.

Can I use both apps at once?

Yes. Some parents use Cubby as their main daily logger for vaccines and family sharing, while also running Huckleberry for sleep prediction features. Both apps can run simultaneously, though logging the same entry twice adds friction over time. Most families settle on one app after a trial period.
