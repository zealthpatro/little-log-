Your baby at 8 months: development, play and what to expect

Development · Updated July 2026 · All articles

Eight months is a turning point in your baby's independence. Many babies are beginning to move around on their own for the first time, pulling up on furniture, mastering sitting and discovering that they can get themselves to things they want. It is thrilling and, for parents, the start of a new chapter in baby-proofing.

This guide covers what most babies are doing around 8 months across motor skills, cognition, language, social development and feeding. As always, these are ranges rather than targets. Babies develop on their own schedule, and most variations are perfectly normal. Talk to your health visitor or GP if you are concerned.

Motor development: on the move

Gross motor development takes a big leap around 8 months for many babies.

  • Crawling in some form. Crawling typically emerges between 7 and 10 months. At 8 months you may see a belly crawl or commando crawl, a classic hands-and-knees crawl, or a bottom shuffle. Some babies skip crawling entirely and move straight toward standing. All of these paths are normal.
  • Pulling to stand beginning. Many 8-month-olds are just starting to pull themselves up to standing using furniture or a person's leg. This often happens before they can lower themselves back down gracefully, leading to some frustrated standing until a grown-up helps.
  • Cruising furniture. A few babies at this age begin taking sideways steps while holding on to furniture, a skill called cruising. It is an important stepping stone toward independent walking.

Now that your baby is mobile, a quick safety check of your home is worthwhile. Secure heavy furniture to walls, move anything fragile or dangerous out of reach, and make sure stair gates are in place.

Fine motor: the pincer grasp begins to develop

Your baby's hands are becoming remarkably more precise.

  • Pincer grasp developing. The pincer grasp, picking up small objects between thumb and forefinger, begins to emerge around 8 months. It is not yet the refined skill it will become, but you may notice your baby attempting to pick up small pieces of food or small objects with more of a thumb-finger approach than before.
  • Deliberately dropping objects. If you have not yet been treated to the drop-and-watch game, it is coming. Your baby is fascinated by gravity and by the response they get from you, and they will happily repeat this experiment many times.
  • Inspecting objects carefully. You may notice your baby holding a toy up, rotating it and examining it from different angles. This curious investigation is a sign of growing cognitive ability.

Cognitive development: object permanence firms up

One of the most significant cognitive milestones of this period is the deepening understanding of object permanence.

  • Understanding that things still exist when hidden. At 8 months, most babies have a firming grasp of object permanence. If you hide a toy under a cloth while they watch, they will lift the cloth to find it. A few months ago, they would have lost interest entirely once the toy disappeared.
  • Peek-a-boo becomes genuinely exciting. Because of this growing understanding, peek-a-boo is now thrilling in a way it was not before. The suspense of waiting for your face to reappear is real to them.
  • Cause and effect exploration. Pressing a button to hear a sound, banging an object to make a noise, dropping something to watch it fall: your baby is running little experiments constantly. This is early scientific thinking.

Language: mamama and dadada

Babbling is becoming richer and more varied at 8 months.

  • Repeated syllable babble. You will likely hear strings of repeated syllables: "mamama", "dadada", "bababa". These are the precursors to first words. At this stage the sounds are not yet deliberately attached to meaning.
  • Varied intonation. Your baby is getting better at varying the pitch and rhythm of their babble in ways that mirror the intonation of real speech. Listen and you can almost hear sentences taking shape.
  • Recognising familiar words. Babies at this age are beginning to build a passive vocabulary, understanding common words like their name, "no", "up" and the names of familiar people, even before they can say any of them.

Social and emotional: separation anxiety begins

The emotional landscape gets more complex around 8 months.

  • Separation anxiety beginning. As your baby's understanding of object permanence grows, so does their awareness of your absence. Many babies begin showing distress when a parent leaves the room around 8 to 10 months. This is healthy attachment in action.
  • Preference for familiar carers. Your baby has a clear, established preference for the people they know best. They may be wary or upset around people they do not see regularly, even if those people are relatives.
  • Social referencing. When something new happens, your baby will increasingly check your face for a cue about how to feel about it. Your calm, reassuring expression genuinely helps them feel safe in novel situations.

Feeding: two meals a day and texture progression

Solid food eating is becoming more established, though milk remains the foundation.

  • Two meals a day. Most families are offering two small solid meals per day at 8 months. Moving toward three meals happens gradually over the coming months.
  • Texture progression. Moving from smooth purees to soft mashed or minced textures helps your baby learn to manage food in their mouth. Offering a variety of textures early supports acceptance of different foods later.
  • Water in a cup. Offering a small amount of water in an open or free-flow cup with meals is appropriate from 6 months. Babies at 8 months are often interested in drinking from a cup, even if most of it ends up on their bib.
  • Finger foods. If your baby's pincer grasp is developing, soft finger foods are worth introducing. Soft pieces of cooked vegetable, banana, or small pieces of soft bread are good starting points.

Play ideas for an 8-month-old

Your baby's growing mobility and curiosity open up a whole new range of play possibilities.

  • Nesting containers. Stacking cups or simple containers that nest inside each other are endlessly fascinating at this age. Filling them, emptying them and banging them together is all great play.
  • Cause-and-effect toys. Simple toys where pressing a button or pulling a lever produces a sound or a pop-up reward are perfect for this stage of development.
  • Peek-a-boo variations. Hide your face behind your hands, behind a cloth, or around a door. The build-up of suspense followed by your reappearance is pure gold for an 8-month-old.
  • Safe exploration of the floor. A baby-proofed area of floor with a variety of safe objects to investigate is one of the best playthings you can offer. Their natural curiosity does the rest.
  • Bath play. The bath is an excellent sensory experience. Pouring cups, splashing and exploring the feel of water are all genuinely enjoyable and developmentally valuable.

When to talk to your health visitor or GP

Most differences in timing are nothing to worry about. Do mention it to your health visitor or GP if your baby:

  • Is not sitting at all with support
  • Is not reaching for or grasping objects
  • Is not babbling with any consonant sounds
  • Does not show any interest in familiar people or social interaction
  • Has lost skills they previously had

Loss of previously acquired skills is always worth raising promptly. For everything else, raise concerns at your next scheduled check or whenever something feels off to you.

Frequently asked questions

Do all babies crawl at 8 months?

No. Crawling typically emerges anywhere between 7 and 10 months, and some babies skip crawling altogether and move directly to standing and walking. What matters is that your baby is exploring their environment in some way.

What is the pincer grasp and when does it develop?

The pincer grasp is the ability to pick up a small object using just the thumb and forefinger. It develops gradually from around 8 months, becoming more refined by 9 to 12 months. Before it arrives, babies use a raking motion to gather objects.

Why does my 8-month-old love dropping things?

Deliberately dropping objects is a form of scientific exploration. Your baby is learning about gravity, cause and effect, and the reactions they can provoke in the people around them. It is completely normal and worth indulging with patience.

When does separation anxiety peak?

Separation anxiety typically begins emerging around 8 months and often peaks somewhere between 10 and 18 months. It is a sign of healthy attachment. Calm, consistent goodbyes and the knowledge that you always return help your baby build confidence over time.

Should I be offering my 8-month-old water?

Yes, small amounts of water in a cup alongside solid meals are appropriate from around 6 months. There is no need to give large volumes. Breast milk or formula remains the primary drink.

Why does peek-a-boo get so much more exciting at this age?

Object permanence is firming up around 8 months. Your baby understands that things continue to exist when hidden from view. That makes peek-a-boo genuinely suspenseful and thrilling, rather than just a pleasant sensory experience.
