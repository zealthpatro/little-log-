Your baby at 7 months: development, play and what to expect

Development · Updated July 2026 · All articles

Seven months in and your baby is becoming a real explorer. They are reaching for things with intention, practising sitting up tall, starting to show a clear sense of who is familiar and who is a stranger, and filling the air with delightful babbling sounds. It is a busy, sociable, curious time.

This guide covers what most babies are doing around 7 months, across motor development, language, social growth, feeding and play. Developmental timelines are ranges, not deadlines. A baby who hits a milestone a few weeks earlier or later than described here is very often perfectly healthy. When in doubt, your health visitor or GP is always the right person to ask.

Motor development: approaching independent sitting

The physical progress between 6 and 9 months is striking. Your baby is building the core strength and balance needed for major new abilities.

  • Approaching independent sitting. Many 7-month-olds can sit with only a little support, and some are reaching brief moments of sitting completely alone. For most babies this milestone solidifies somewhere between 6 and 9 months, so wherever your baby is in that range is fine.
  • Pivoting on the tummy. In preparation for crawling, babies at this age often begin pivoting in a circle on their tummy to get a different view or reach an object just out of range. It is an early and important form of mobility.
  • Pre-crawling exploration. Some babies begin rocking on hands and knees or doing a commando-style belly crawl. Others stay on the tummy and work up to crawling a bit later. The timeline for crawling is broad.

Fine motor: raking grasp and hand-to-hand transfer

Your baby's hands are becoming more skilled with each week.

  • Raking grasp. At this age babies typically use a raking motion, sweeping all four fingers toward their palm to pick up small objects. This is a normal and expected stage before the precise pincer grasp develops.
  • Transferring objects hand to hand. Passing a toy from one hand to the other is a meaningful milestone that shows growing bilateral coordination and intentional control.
  • Throwing and dropping. Many 7-month-olds discover the entertaining quality of dropping or throwing objects repeatedly. This is genuinely educational, not just maddening. They are learning about gravity and cause and effect.

Social development: stranger awareness begins

One of the more emotionally loaded developments of this period is the emergence of stranger awareness.

  • Stranger awareness beginning. Your baby is now sophisticated enough to distinguish clearly between familiar and unfamiliar people. When someone new gets too close, you may notice your baby clinging to you, crying, or going very quiet and watchful. This is a sign of healthy attachment.
  • Responding to their own name. Between 6 and 9 months most babies reliably turn when they hear their name called. If your baby is starting to do this, it is a lovely sign of growing awareness of themselves as a distinct person.
  • Turn-taking in babble. Your baby is becoming better at the back-and-forth rhythm of conversation. They vocalise, pause, listen to you respond, then vocalise again. These proto-conversations are joyful and developmentally important.

Language: consonant babble arrives

Language development at 7 months is exciting. You will likely hear a real flowering of new sounds.

  • Consonant babbling. Sounds like "ba", "da" and "ma" are starting to appear. Your baby is experimenting with consonant-vowel combinations, which is the structural basis of all the words to come. At this stage the sounds are not yet attached to meaning.
  • Varied pitch and volume. Babies at this age are discovering that they can vary how loud or quiet they are, and how high or low their voice sounds. You may notice singing-like vocalisations and sudden shouts of delight.
  • Responding to tone. Your baby is becoming better at reading the emotional tone of your voice. A warm, encouraging tone and a concerned tone will produce noticeably different reactions.

Feeding: one to two small meals a day

If you began introducing solid foods around 6 months, your baby is now about one to two months into the weaning journey.

  • Milk remains primary. At 7 months, breast milk or formula is still the main source of calories and nutrition. Solid meals are about learning and exploration as much as nutrition at this stage.
  • One to two small meals. Most families offering solids from 6 months are giving one or two small meals per day by 7 months. There is no single right pace; follow your baby's cues.
  • Texture progression. Smooth purees are fine, but many babies are beginning to manage slightly lumpier or mashed textures. Offering a variety of tastes early is associated with better food acceptance later.

If you have not yet started solid foods, 7 months is still a fine time to begin. There is no benefit to delaying beyond 6 months for a healthy, full-term baby.

Sleep: settling into a pattern

Sleep at 7 months varies enormously between babies, but many families start to notice a more predictable rhythm emerging.

  • Two or three naps. Most 7-month-olds take two or three naps during the day. The transition from three naps to two often happens somewhere between 6 and 9 months.
  • Night waking is still common. Many babies at this age continue to wake once or twice overnight. This is biologically normal, not a sign of a problem.
  • Separation at bedtime. As stranger and separation awareness grows, some babies become harder to settle at bedtime. A calm, consistent routine helps enormously.

Play ideas for a 7-month-old

Your 7-month-old is ready for more interactive, exploratory play.

  • Peek-a-boo. Your baby is beginning to understand that things continue to exist even when hidden, making peek-a-boo genuinely thrilling rather than just confusing. Cover your face and reveal it for squeals of delight.
  • Passing toys. Offer a toy and watch your baby take it, examine it, and transfer it to the other hand. Offer it back. This simple exchange is great for hand skills and social interaction.
  • Texture exploration baskets. Collect a range of safe objects with different textures, such as a wooden ring, a soft cloth, a smooth spoon and a crinkle toy. Let your baby investigate each one freely.
  • Floor time with mirrors. A baby-safe floor mirror lets your baby practise sitting while enjoying the fascinating face in the reflection.
  • Simple songs with actions. Songs with hand movements like "Twinkle Twinkle" or "The Wheels on the Bus" delight babies at this age and quietly build language connections.

When to talk to your health visitor or GP

Most differences in developmental timing are completely normal. Do mention it to your health visitor or GP if your baby:

  • Is not babbling or making any consonant sounds
  • Does not reach for objects or show interest in toys
  • Cannot sit with any support at all
  • Does not respond to their name at all by 9 months
  • Shows no interest in familiar people or no social response to caregivers

If something feels off to you, raise it. You know your baby better than anyone, and early support makes a genuine difference.

Frequently asked questions

Should my 7-month-old be sitting independently?

Many babies approach independent sitting around 7 months, but the full milestone of sitting without any support is typically achieved between 6 and 9 months. At 7 months it is completely normal for your baby to still need a hand nearby or a cushion behind them.

What is a raking grasp?

A raking grasp is when a baby uses all four fingers together to drag an object toward them, like a rake. It appears around 5 to 7 months and gradually gives way to the more precise pincer grasp by around 9 to 12 months.

Is it normal for a 7-month-old to be shy with strangers?

Yes. Stranger awareness often begins to emerge between 6 and 9 months. Your baby is becoming more aware of who is and is not familiar, and may react with caution or upset when someone unfamiliar gets close. This is a healthy sign of secure attachment.

What should I be feeding my 7-month-old?

At 7 months, breast milk or formula remains the primary source of nutrition, with solid foods introduced alongside from around 6 months. Most families are offering one or two small meals a day at this point, with smooth purees or appropriately soft textures.

When do babies start responding to their name?

Consistently turning toward their own name is a milestone most babies achieve between 6 and 9 months. At 7 months some babies respond reliably; others are still getting there. If your baby is not responding to their name at all by 9 months, mention it to your health visitor.

What play activities are best for a 7-month-old?

Simple activities like peek-a-boo, offering toys to transfer between hands, exploring different textures and shapes, and floor time with safe objects are all wonderful at this age. Your presence and interaction are the most valuable ingredient in any of them.
